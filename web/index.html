Core Pricing in Learned Assignment Markets

When a ride-hailing platform matches riders to drivers using predicted (noisy) surplus values, how much do the optimal matching and the stable (core) prices change with prediction error, can riders or drivers gain by misreporting, and which stable price should the platform choose?

A predicted surplus is modelled as the true surplus plus Gaussian error. Everything below is computed in your browser.

1Surplus matrix and optimal matching

Entry aij is rider i's value minus driver j's cost. The assignment LP picks the matching with the largest total. Matched pairs are shaded.

2Core prices

Each bar is the range of one agent's share over all core (stable) price vectors, the optimal solutions of the dual LP. RO is the rider-optimal point, DO the driver-optimal point and MID their average.

3Prediction error

Each draw adds N(0, σ2) error to every entry, re-solves on the estimate and compares with the truth (seed 1).

4Misreporting

One rider shifts their own row, or one driver their own column, by −2, −1, 1 or 2. Gain is the true payoff under the false report minus the truthful payoff. Select a cell for details.

5Which price should the platform choose?

A zero-sum game: the platform picks RO, MID or DO, a manipulator picks the riders' or the drivers' side, and the payoff is the best gain from section 4. The platform minimises.
